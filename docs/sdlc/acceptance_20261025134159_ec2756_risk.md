## Risk register

### RAF72C-001 — Sign-in wireframe and recovery navigation diverge

**Category:** UI and navigation · **Proposed priority:** should · **Requirements:** REQ-001, REQ-002, REQ-003, REQ-004, REQ-005, REQ-006, REQ-007, REQ-009, REQ-019 · **Evidence:** S, A, K1–K5

If sign-in appearance or navigation diverges from the supplied image, visitors may miss or fail to reach recovery. Verify the white page, thin outlined rectangular card, bold Sign in heading, Email address/name@example.com field, masked Password/******** field, blue underlined Forgot password? link and its reset-request target, dark Sign in button, and No account? Contact your administrator. footer on the server-rendered page.

### RAF72C-002 — Synthetic sign-in and reset flow do not work end to end

**Category:** Recovery flow · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-008, REQ-009, REQ-010, REQ-015, REQ-020 · **Evidence:** S, K1–K5

If synthetic sign-in, reset-request submission, link issuance, or password update does not form a usable flow, an owner may be unable to recover access. Exercise a synthetic account's sign-in and active session, request a reset, inspect its locally observable link in the in-memory sink rather than a public response, complete a valid reset, and sign in with the new password.

### RAF72C-003 — Public reset response reveals account existence

**Category:** Response privacy · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-011 · **Evidence:** K1–K5

If known and unknown identifiers receive different public reset-request outcomes, an observer could enumerate accounts. Compare otherwise equivalent public response content, message, and outcome for known and unknown identifiers, including absence of any reset link; do not infer an unknown-account numeric quota.

### RAF72C-004 — Reset-request limit is bypassed or discloses account status

**Category:** Request throttling · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-011, REQ-012 · **Evidence:** K1–K5

If known-account requests are counted incorrectly, excess links may be issued; if the public limit response changes by account existence, it may disclose status. For one known synthetic account, verify five accepted requests and a blocked sixth within a rolling hour, then acceptance outside that window, while checking public-response parity. Rolling-hour accounting is predecessor assumption A4, not a literal standard quote; no unknown-account quota is specified.

### RAF72C-005 — Reset link can be replayed or has incorrect expiry

**Category:** Token lifecycle · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-010, REQ-013, REQ-014, REQ-015 · **Evidence:** S, K1–K5

If a used or stale link succeeds, an invalid password change may occur; if expiry is computed incorrectly, a timely valid reset may fail. Verify usable issuance, successful password update before 30 minutes from issuance, rejected replay without password change, and rejected use at or after that 30-minute boundary without password change. The at-or-after boundary is predecessor assumption A5.

### RAF72C-006 — Reset leaves existing sessions authenticated

**Category:** Session invalidation · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-008, REQ-016 · **Evidence:** S, K1–K5

If any previously active session survives a successful reset, prior access to the account remains available. Sign in to the same synthetic account in multiple sessions, complete a reset, and verify that every previously active session is no longer authenticated.

### RAF72C-007 — Owner notification missing or demo link leaks publicly

**Category:** Notification and containment · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-010, REQ-017, REQ-021 · **Evidence:** S, K1–K5

If a successful reset has no owner-addressed notification, the owner lacks the required notice; if demo sink/link data appears in a public reset response, it may expose a recovery path. Inspect an owner-addressed in-memory notification for every successful reset, without assuming external delivery, and verify that public reset-request responses contain no link.

### RAF72C-008 — Pytest coverage misses recovery regressions

**Category:** Regression verification · **Proposed priority:** should · **Requirements:** REQ-011, REQ-012, REQ-013, REQ-014, REQ-015, REQ-016, REQ-017, REQ-022 · **Evidence:** S, K1–K5

If automated scenarios omit critical recovery behavior, response parity, throttling, token, session, or notification regressions may go undetected. Run pytest scenarios covering known/unknown response parity, five-per-hour known-account requests, single use, pre-expiry usability and expiry from issuance, password update, all-session invalidation, and owner notification.

### RAF72C-009 — Demo architecture or production-readiness boundary is misrepresented

**Category:** Runtime and scope · **Proposed priority:** must · **Requirements:** REQ-018, REQ-019, REQ-020, REQ-021, REQ-023, REQ-024 · **Evidence:** S

If runtime, rendering, accounts, or notifications depart from the local-demo scope, or production readiness/deployment is implied, the delivery may mislead users or exceed its authorization. Verify Python 3.12 with Flask and server-rendered sign-in/reset pages, synthetic-only accounts, an in-memory notification sink with no external delivery, explicit production limitations, and no deployment or newly added deployment workflows.

## Assumptions

1. **A1:** A locally demonstrable synthetic sign-in and active session suffice; exact sign-in result and password policy remain unspecified.
2. **A2:** The reset-request identifier is an email, and reset pages are server-rendered; their exact styling and error wording are unspecified. Only the sign-in wireframe supplies visual detail.
3. **A3:** Known-account reset links and successful-reset notifications are observable in an in-memory sink for local demonstration and tests; no external delivery is assumed, and public reset-request responses contain no links.
4. **A4:** Interpret five requests per account per hour as a rolling hour for known synthetic accounts; do not infer an unknown-account numeric quota.
5. **A5:** Interpret expiry as invalid at or after 30 minutes measured from link issuance; this boundary convention is not a quotation of ACC-STD-02.
6. No implementation, test results, or measured likelihood/control-effectiveness evidence was supplied. ## Open questions (nonblocking)

- What exact sign-in result and password policy should be used? A1 permits a minimal locally demonstrable sign-in/session flow without inventing additional policy.
- How should unknown identifiers be treated under the five-per-account-per-hour limit? A4 covers known synthetic accounts only; public responses must remain indistinguishable.
- What presentation and error wording should reset-request and new-password pages use? A2 leaves reset-page specifics open; the supplied wireframe covers sign-in only.
- How should reset links be delivered without production notification infrastructure? A3 uses an in-memory sink for local observation and tests, never a public reset-request response.
